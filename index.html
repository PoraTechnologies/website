The problem

The separator is holding electrolysis back

Green hydrogen lives or dies on the cost of the machine that makes it. Inside today's electrolyzers, the membrane or diaphragm that keeps hydrogen and oxygen apart is also a major source of cost, degradation, and operating constraints.

COST

Expensive, fragile components

Membranes, diaphragms, and the hardware built around them drive stack cost and limit lifetime. Replacing them means downtime and money.

CROSSOVER

Gas mixing at partial load

When renewable power dips, gas crossover through the separator forces conventional systems to throttle or shut down for safety.

COMPLEXITY

Complex stacks, costly plants

Every extra layer in the stack multiplies manufacturing steps, sealing surfaces, and failure modes at the plant level.

The technology

Electrodes that breathe gas out of the cell

Our mixed-wettability, gas-breathing electrodes hold the electrolyte on one side and release gas on the other. Hydrogen and oxygen each leave through the back of their own electrode, so they never meet, and no membrane is needed to keep them apart.

  • 01

    Capillarity does the separation

    Engineered wettability keeps the electrolyte inside the electrode while opening dry pathways for gas. The gas produced at the catalyst escapes through the back, not into the liquid.

  • 02

    Bubble-free, membrane-free

    No bubbles in the electrolyte gap means low resistance and stable operation. No membrane means one less thing to buy, seal, maintain, and replace.

  • 03

    Built on abundant materials

    Nickel-based electrodes and mature alkaline chemistry. No iridium, no titanium plates, no exotic supply chains standing between us and gigawatt scale.

Where it lands

Built for industries that need hydrogen at scale

Cheap, dependable electrolysis unlocks decarbonization where hydrogen is the feedstock, not the fuel.

Green ammonia

Fertilizer production is one of the largest hydrogen consumers on Earth. Lower-cost electrolysis moves green ammonia from pilot to default.

DRI

Green steel

Direct reduced iron with hydrogen replaces coal in steelmaking. Electrolyzer capex is the lever that makes the business case close.

e-FUELS

Refineries and synthetic fuel plants need continuous, high-purity hydrogen. Simpler stacks mean higher uptime and lower levelized cost.
